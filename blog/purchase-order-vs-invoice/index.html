Purchase Order vs Invoice: What Is the Difference?

A purchase order starts from the buyer, while an invoice starts from the seller. Together they help both parties verify what was ordered and what is being billed.

Who issues each document

The buyer issues the purchase order. The supplier issues the invoice. This difference reflects their roles in the transaction.

When they are issued

A purchase order is normally sent before delivery or work begins. An invoice is normally issued after goods or services are supplied, or at an agreed milestone.

What each document does

The purchase order authorizes and defines the purchase. The invoice requests payment and records the amount due.

Matching example

A buyer issues PO-310 for 10 chairs at $120 each, total $1,200 before tax. The supplier delivers the chairs and sends an invoice referencing PO-310. The buyer checks that quantities and prices match before payment.

Why references matter

Putting the purchase-order number on the invoice helps the customer route it to the correct approval and budget. Missing references are a common reason invoices are returned.

Can one exist without the other?

Yes. Many small transactions use only an invoice, while larger organizations may refuse to pay without a valid purchase order. Confirm the customer’s process before starting work.

Important: Document, accounting and tax requirements vary by location and circumstance. This guide provides general information and is not legal, tax or accounting advice.

Frequently asked questions

Does a PO mean the invoice is paid?

No. It authorizes the purchase but does not confirm payment.

Should an invoice copy the PO exactly?

It should match agreed items and prices, while showing actual supplied quantities and applicable tax.

Can several invoices reference one PO?

Yes, for staged deliveries or recurring billing, provided the PO permits it and remaining value is tracked.
