How Long Should a Quotation Be Valid?

A validity period tells the customer how long the offered price and conditions remain open for acceptance.

Why quotations expire

Material costs, exchange rates, staff availability and supplier prices can change. An expiry date prevents an old quotation from being accepted after the assumptions behind it are no longer realistic.

Common periods

Fourteen, thirty and sixty days are common examples, but there is no universal period. Shorter periods may suit volatile costs or limited availability. Longer periods may suit stable, standardized services.

Match the sales cycle

A large organization may need several weeks for approvals. A seven-day validity period could create unnecessary revisions if the normal decision cycle is thirty days.

Write the date clearly

State an exact expiry date, for example “Valid until August 31, 2026.” This is clearer than “valid for thirty days” when documents are forwarded or reviewed later.

After expiry

You can extend the quotation in writing or issue a revised version. Recheck prices, capacity, taxes and delivery dates before confirming an extension.

Deposits and reservations

If acceptance reserves scarce capacity, explain whether a deposit is required and when the booking becomes confirmed. Quotation acceptance alone may not be enough if your terms require payment.

Important: Document, accounting and tax requirements vary by location and circumstance. This guide provides general information and is not legal, tax or accounting advice.

Frequently asked questions

Does an expired quote automatically become invalid?

That is the intended effect, but contract interpretation can vary. Communicate clearly before proceeding.

Can I honor an expired quote anyway?

Yes, after confirming that you can still supply on the same terms.

Should every quote have an expiry date?

It is strongly recommended because it avoids open-ended pricing commitments.
