Invoice Email Template and Sending Checklist

The invoice email should make the next action obvious: review the attached invoice and pay by the stated date.

Subject line

Use a searchable subject such as “Invoice INV-1042 from Bright Studio — due August 15, 2026.” Include the invoice number and business name.

Opening sentence

State what the invoice covers: “Please find attached invoice INV-1042 for the July website maintenance service.”

Amount and due date

Repeat the amount and exact due date in the email body. For example: “The total due is $850 by August 15, 2026.”

Payment instructions

Briefly mention the available payment method and tell the customer where full details appear. Avoid copying sensitive banking information into several messages unnecessarily.

Attachment check

Attach the correct PDF before sending. Open it once to confirm the file is readable and belongs to the intended customer.

Concise template

Subject: Invoice INV-1042 — due August 15, 2026. Body: “Hello [Name], please find attached invoice INV-1042 for [service]. The total of $850 is due August 15, 2026. Payment instructions are included on the invoice. Please let me know if your accounts team needs any additional information. Thank you.”

Important: Document, accounting and tax requirements vary by location and circumstance. This guide provides general information and is not legal, tax or accounting advice.

Frequently asked questions

Should I write a long invoice email?

No. Keep it concise while including the invoice number, purpose, amount and due date.

Should I ask for confirmation?

You can ask the customer to confirm receipt when the invoice is important or time-sensitive.

Can I send an invoice in the email body only?

A separate invoice document is usually easier to record, forward and process.
