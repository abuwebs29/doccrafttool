Quotation vs Estimate: Key Differences

Both documents communicate an expected price before work begins, but they differ in certainty and purpose.

What a quotation is

A quotation usually offers a defined scope at a stated price for a limited period. Once accepted, it may form part of the agreement between seller and customer.

What an estimate is

An estimate is an informed approximation based on available information. It is useful when final quantities, time or conditions cannot yet be known.

Price certainty

A quote generally carries more price certainty. An estimate allows more adjustment, but the basis and possible range should be explained rather than leaving the customer with an open-ended figure.

Example

A designer may quote $2,400 for a defined five-page website. A repair company may estimate $800–$1,100 because hidden damage cannot be assessed until work starts.

When to use each

Use a quotation when scope and inputs are sufficiently clear. Use an estimate when uncertainty is genuine, then update the customer before exceeding the expected range.

Document changes

If scope changes after acceptance, record the new work and price in writing. Do not rely on the label “estimate” to justify unexplained increases.

Important: Document, accounting and tax requirements vary by location and circumstance. This guide provides general information and is not legal, tax or accounting advice.

Frequently asked questions

Can an estimate become a final quote?

Yes, once enough information is available to define the scope and price.

Should estimates include an expiry date?

It is helpful when costs or availability may change.

Which document should a customer sign?

Either can include acknowledgment, but a quotation is more commonly presented for formal acceptance.
