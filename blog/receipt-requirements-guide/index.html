What Information Should a Receipt Include?

A receipt confirms that payment was received. It should identify the transaction clearly enough for both parties to keep reliable records.

Seller information

Include the business or seller name and contact details. Add registration and tax identifiers when required where you operate.

Transaction date

State the date payment was received. If the service or delivery date is different and relevant, record that separately.

Receipt number

Use a unique receipt number so the transaction can be found later. A simple sequential system is usually enough for a small business.

Items or services

List what the customer paid for, with quantities and prices where appropriate. Avoid a generic description such as “payment” when a more specific description is available.

Amount and payment method

Show the subtotal, tax, discount and total paid. Record whether the customer paid by cash, card, bank transfer or another method. Do not place sensitive full card details on the receipt.

Payment status

Use clear wording such as “Paid in full.” For a partial payment, state the amount received and the remaining balance so the receipt is not mistaken for full settlement.

Important: Document, accounting and tax requirements vary by location and circumstance. This guide provides general information and is not legal, tax or accounting advice.

Frequently asked questions

Is a receipt the same as an invoice?

No. An invoice requests payment, while a receipt confirms that payment was received.

Does a receipt need the customer name?

It is useful for business transactions and may be required in some situations, but requirements vary.

Can I issue a digital receipt?

Yes in many contexts, provided it contains the necessary information and can be retained by the customer.
