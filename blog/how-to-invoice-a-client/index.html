How to Invoice a Client Professionally

A professional invoice makes it easy for a client to understand what they bought, approve the charge and pay on time.

Confirm the commercial details

Before drafting the invoice, confirm the legal or trading name of the client, the billing contact, purchase-order reference if required, agreed price, currency, tax treatment and payment deadline.

Describe the work clearly

Use line items that match the proposal, contract or timesheet. “Website design — milestone 2” is more useful than “services.” Add dates, quantities or hours when they help the client verify the charge.

Calculate the total

List the subtotal, any discount, tax and final amount due. For example, a $1,000 service with a $100 discount has a $900 subtotal before applicable tax. Check both the arithmetic and the tax basis.

Add payment instructions

State the due date as an exact date and explain how to pay. Include the account name, bank details or payment link, and ask the client to use the invoice number as the payment reference.

Send it to the right person

Address the invoice to the billing contact and copy the project contact only when useful. Use a direct subject line such as “Invoice INV-1042 — Website design milestone 2.”

Follow up professionally

Send a brief reminder shortly before or after the due date. Reference the invoice number, amount and due date, attach the invoice again and ask whether anything is blocking approval. Keep the tone factual rather than accusatory.

Important: Document, accounting and tax requirements vary by location and circumstance. This guide provides general information and is not legal, tax or accounting advice.

Frequently asked questions

When should I send an invoice?

Send it at the milestone or date agreed with the client, ideally as soon as the billable work is complete.

Should I send invoices as PDF files?

PDF is widely used because it preserves formatting. Also keep an editable source or record for your own files.

What should the email say?

State what the invoice covers, the amount, due date and where the invoice is attached or linked.
