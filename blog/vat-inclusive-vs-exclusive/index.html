VAT Inclusive vs VAT Exclusive Prices

A VAT-exclusive price is the amount before tax. A VAT-inclusive price is the final amount after VAT has been included.

VAT-exclusive price

If a service is listed at $1,000 excluding 5% VAT, the VAT is $50 and the customer pays $1,050.

VAT-inclusive price

If the advertised total is $1,050 including 5% VAT, the underlying net amount is $1,000 and the VAT portion is $50.

Why wording matters

Writing only “$1,000” can be ambiguous. State “$1,000 excluding VAT” or “$1,000 including VAT” wherever tax treatment affects the amount payable.

Business and consumer pricing

Display requirements may differ depending on whether customers are businesses or consumers and where the transaction occurs. Check applicable pricing and tax rules.

Quotations and invoices

Use the same basis across your quotation, order and invoice. A quote presented as tax-inclusive should not unexpectedly become tax-exclusive at invoicing.

Comparing supplier prices

When comparing offers, convert them to the same basis. A tax-exclusive quote may look cheaper than a tax-inclusive quote even when the final totals are equal.

Important: Document, accounting and tax requirements vary by location and circumstance. This guide provides general information and is not legal, tax or accounting advice.

Frequently asked questions

Does inclusive mean tax is already paid?

It means the displayed price contains the tax amount; payment and reporting still occur through the transaction.

How do I extract VAT from an inclusive price?

Divide the gross amount by 1 plus the VAT rate to find the net amount, then subtract.

Should invoices show VAT separately?

Often yes where VAT applies, but exact invoice requirements vary by jurisdiction.
