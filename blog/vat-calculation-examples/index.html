VAT Calculation Examples for Common Rates

Worked examples make it easier to see the difference between adding VAT and extracting VAT from a tax-inclusive total.

5% example

For a $2,000 net amount, VAT is $2,000 × 0.05 = $100. The gross total is $2,100. If $2,100 already includes 5% VAT, divide by 1.05 to recover the $2,000 net amount.

10% example

For a $500 net amount, VAT is $50 and the gross total is $550. From a $550 inclusive amount, $550 ÷ 1.10 gives $500 net.

15% example

For a $800 net amount, VAT is $120 and gross is $920. From $920 inclusive, divide by 1.15 to get $800 net.

20% example

For a $1,250 net amount, VAT is $250 and gross is $1,500. From $1,500 inclusive, divide by 1.20 to get $1,250 net.

Multiple line items

You may calculate VAT per line or on a taxable subtotal depending on local rules and system settings. Keep the method consistent and review rounding differences.

Different rates on one invoice

Some invoices contain standard-rated, zero-rated or exempt items. Separate the tax treatment and do not apply one rate to the entire total unless appropriate.

Important: Document, accounting and tax requirements vary by location and circumstance. This guide provides general information and is not legal, tax or accounting advice.

Frequently asked questions

What is the fastest way to add VAT?

Multiply the net amount by 1 plus the rate, such as 1.05 for 5%.

What is the fastest way to remove VAT?

Divide the gross amount by 1 plus the rate.

Are zero-rated and exempt the same?

No. They may both show no tax charged, but their legal and reporting treatment can differ.
