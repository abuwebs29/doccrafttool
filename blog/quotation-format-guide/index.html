Professional Quotation Format: Sections and Examples

A quotation presents the proposed price and conditions for work before the customer accepts. A structured format makes comparison and approval easier.

Header and reference

Include your business details, the customer details, quotation number, issue date and expiry date. Use a unique quotation reference just as you would use an invoice number.

Scope of supply

Describe each product, service or deliverable. Include quantities, units, assumptions and exclusions that affect price. Clear scope reduces later disagreements.

Price summary

Show line totals, subtotal, discount, tax and total quoted amount. State the currency. For example, two design workshops at $600 each produce a $1,200 line total before tax.

Validity period

State how long the quotation remains valid, such as fourteen or thirty days. This protects against indefinite commitments when costs or availability can change.

Delivery and payment terms

Explain the expected delivery schedule, deposit, milestone payments and final payment timing. Mention whether travel, shipping or third-party costs are included.

Acceptance

Provide a simple acceptance method, such as signing the quotation or replying with written approval. Clarify that work begins only after acceptance and any required deposit.

Important: Document, accounting and tax requirements vary by location and circumstance. This guide provides general information and is not legal, tax or accounting advice.

Frequently asked questions

Is a quotation legally binding?

It can become binding when accepted, depending on its wording and applicable law. Use clear terms and seek advice for significant contracts.

How long should a quote be valid?

Choose a period that reflects price stability, capacity and customer decision time.

Can I change a quote after acceptance?

Material changes should be agreed and documented, often through a revised quotation or change order.
