How to Make a Rent Receipt

A rent receipt provides a written record that rent was received for a particular property and rental period.

Identify the parties

Include the landlord or property manager name and the tenant name. Use contact details or an address where appropriate.

Identify the property

State the rental property address or unit number. This is particularly important when a landlord manages several properties.

State the rental period

Write the month or date range covered, such as “Rent for August 1–31, 2026.” The payment date alone does not always show which period the payment applies to.

Record the amount

Show the amount received and currency. Separate rent from utilities, parking, late fees or other charges when several amounts are collected together.

Payment method and balance

Record whether payment was by cash, bank transfer, check or another method. For a partial payment, show the remaining rent balance.

Signatures and local rules

A signature or digital confirmation can strengthen the record. Requirements for rent receipts vary by location, so landlords and tenants should check applicable housing and tax rules.

Important: Document, accounting and tax requirements vary by location and circumstance. This guide provides general information and is not legal, tax or accounting advice.

Frequently asked questions

Should I issue a rent receipt for bank transfers?

It can still be useful because it links the payment to a specific property and rental period.

Can one receipt cover several months?

Yes, but list the covered months and amount allocated to each period.

Does a rent receipt replace a lease?

No. It records payment but does not replace the tenancy agreement.
