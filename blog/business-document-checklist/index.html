Business Basics

Small Business Document Checklist

A simple document trail helps a small business understand what was offered, ordered, delivered, billed and paid.

Before the sale

Use a quotation or proposal to define scope, price, timing, assumptions and validity. Keep written customer acceptance.

When purchasing

Issue or retain purchase orders when they are part of the buying process. Record approvals and supplier acknowledgments.

During delivery

Keep delivery notes, work logs, timesheets or completion approvals when they help prove what was supplied.

When billing

Create an invoice with a unique number, accurate customer details, line items, taxes, due date and payment instructions.

After payment

Issue or retain a receipt and keep the related bank or payment-platform record. Mark the invoice paid in your register.

Retention and privacy

Store records securely for the period required where you operate. Limit access, avoid collecting unnecessary personal data and maintain backups of important business records.

Important: Document, accounting and tax requirements vary by location and circumstance. This guide provides general information and is not legal, tax or accounting advice.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need every document for every transaction?

No. The right set depends on transaction value, risk, customer process and legal requirements.

Can digital records be enough?

Often yes, provided they are reliable, accessible and retained as required.

What is the minimum useful sequence?

For many services: accepted quote or agreement, invoice, payment record and receipt.
