Proof of Payment vs Receipt: What Is the Difference?

Proof of payment shows that money moved. A receipt is a seller-issued record confirming what the payment was for. They overlap, but they are not always interchangeable.

What proof of payment shows

A bank transfer confirmation, card statement or payment-platform record may show the payer, payee, date and amount. It demonstrates that a payment instruction was processed or recorded.

What a receipt shows

A receipt links the payment to specific goods or services. It normally identifies the seller, transaction, items, taxes, total and payment status.

Why both may be needed

A transfer confirmation may not explain which invoice was paid, while a receipt may not independently prove that a bank completed the transfer. Businesses often retain both for reconciliation.

Example

A customer transfers $750 and receives a bank confirmation. The seller then issues receipt RCPT-205 stating that $750 was received for invoice INV-198. Together, the records connect the money movement to the commercial transaction.

Disputes and reversals

Some payment records can later be reversed, refunded or disputed. A receipt should reflect the status known when it was issued and should not misrepresent a pending payment as final.

Record keeping

Store the receipt with the related invoice and payment reference. Avoid sharing screenshots that expose unrelated balances, account numbers or personal transactions.

Important: Document, accounting and tax requirements vary by location and circumstance. This guide provides general information and is not legal, tax or accounting advice.

Frequently asked questions

Can a bank statement replace a receipt?

It may prove a payment amount, but it often does not describe the goods or services purchased.

Who issues the receipt?

Normally the seller or payment recipient issues it.

Should I issue a receipt for partial payment?

Yes, but state the amount received and remaining balance clearly.
