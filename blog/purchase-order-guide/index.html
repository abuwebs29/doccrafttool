Purchase Orders

Purchase Order Guide for Small Businesses

A purchase order is a buyer-issued document that authorizes a supplier to provide specified goods or services under stated conditions.

Purpose of a purchase order

The purchase order records what the buyer wants, the agreed price, delivery requirements and billing instructions. It creates a reference used by purchasing, receiving and accounts payable.

Core information

Include the buyer and supplier details, purchase-order number, issue date, items, quantities, unit prices, tax, total, delivery address, requested date and payment terms.

Approval control

Businesses often require internal approval before a purchase order is sent. This helps control spending and prevents employees from committing the business without authority.

Supplier confirmation

Ask the supplier to confirm availability, pricing and delivery. A purchase order does not guarantee that the supplier can meet every requested term until they accept or acknowledge it.

Receiving and matching

When goods or services arrive, compare them with the purchase order. Accounts payable may then match the purchase order, delivery record and supplier invoice before payment.

Changes and cancellations

Document changes through a revised purchase order or written amendment. Do not alter quantities or prices informally when several teams rely on the original reference.

Important: Document, accounting and tax requirements vary by location and circumstance. This guide provides general information and is not legal, tax or accounting advice.

Frequently asked questions

Who creates a purchase order?

The buyer creates it and sends it to the supplier.

Is a purchase order the same as an invoice?

No. A purchase order authorizes a purchase; an invoice requests payment after supply.

Do very small businesses need purchase orders?

They are optional in many situations but useful for controlling and documenting spending.
