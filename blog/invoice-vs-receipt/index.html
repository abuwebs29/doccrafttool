Invoice vs Receipt: What Is the Difference?

Learn when to issue an invoice, when to issue a receipt and why businesses often need both.

The main difference

An invoice requests payment, while a receipt confirms that payment has already been made. The documents may contain similar information, but they are issued at different stages of a transaction.

When to issue an invoice

Issue an invoice after delivering goods or services when the customer still owes payment. The invoice should explain what is due and when it must be paid.

When to issue a receipt

Issue a receipt after receiving payment. It acts as evidence that the transaction was completed and should show the amount paid, payment date and relevant item details.

Can one replace the other?

Usually not. An invoice is part of the collection process; a receipt is proof of payment. In some cash transactions, a sales receipt can be the main record because payment and delivery happen together.

Simple example

A designer finishes a project and sends an invoice for $800. After the client pays the $800, the designer issues a receipt confirming the payment.
