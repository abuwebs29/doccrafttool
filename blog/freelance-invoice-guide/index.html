Freelance Invoice Guide: What to Include and How to Get Paid

Freelancers need invoices that are easy to approve and easy to pay. A consistent process also saves time at the end of every project.

Freelancer details

Include your name or business name, contact information and any registration or tax details required in your jurisdiction. Use the same identity that appears in your contract and payment account.

Client details

Use the client’s correct legal or trading name and billing address. Ask whether a purchase-order number, vendor ID or department name is needed before you submit the invoice.

Scope and line items

Break the work into recognizable items such as “Logo design — final files” or “Copywriting — 5 landing pages.” If you charge hourly, show the hours and rate. If you charge a fixed fee, show the agreed milestone or deliverable.

Deposits and milestones

For larger projects, a deposit and milestone schedule can reduce risk. A $3,000 project might be billed as a $1,000 deposit, $1,000 at draft approval and $1,000 on final delivery. Match each invoice to the agreed stage.

Expenses and taxes

List reimbursable expenses separately and attach evidence when the client requires it. Apply tax only when appropriate and clearly show whether rates include or exclude tax.

Payment routine

Send invoices promptly, keep a register of due dates and follow up consistently. A polite reminder system is more effective than waiting several weeks and then sending an urgent message.

Important: Document, accounting and tax requirements vary by location and circumstance. This guide provides general information and is not legal, tax or accounting advice.

Frequently asked questions

Can a freelancer invoice without a company?

Often yes, using their personal or sole-trader details, but local registration and tax rules vary.

Should freelancers request a deposit?

A deposit can reduce risk on larger or customized projects when agreed before work starts.

What currency should I use?

Use the currency agreed with the client and state it clearly on the invoice.
