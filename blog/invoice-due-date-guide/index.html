How to Set an Invoice Due Date

The due date is one of the most important fields on an invoice. It converts a general request for payment into a specific deadline.

Start with the agreement

Use the timing stated in the proposal, contract or purchase order. Do not wait until invoicing to introduce a shorter deadline that the customer did not accept.

Prefer a specific date

“Due August 15, 2026” is clearer than “Net 15” because the customer does not need to calculate the date. You can include both, for example “Net 15 — due August 15, 2026.”

Account for approval time

Large customers may need internal approval, vendor setup or purchase-order matching. Ask about their process before agreeing to a deadline that their system cannot meet.

Weekends and holidays

Consider whether the due date falls on a weekend or public holiday. A nearby business day can be easier for both sides, especially when bank processing is involved.

Deposits and staged payments

For long projects, use several due dates tied to milestones instead of one large final invoice. This supports cash flow and limits the unpaid balance at any one time.

Reminder schedule

Record the due date and plan reminders. A friendly reminder a few days before the deadline and a factual follow-up after it passes can prevent an invoice from being overlooked.

Important: Document, accounting and tax requirements vary by location and circumstance. This guide provides general information and is not legal, tax or accounting advice.

Frequently asked questions

Is the invoice date the same as the due date?

No. The invoice date is when the invoice is issued; the due date is the payment deadline.

Can a due date be the same day?

Yes, when “due on receipt” was agreed, but it may be impractical for customers with approval processes.

What if no payment term was agreed?

Ask the customer to confirm a reasonable deadline and document it in writing.
