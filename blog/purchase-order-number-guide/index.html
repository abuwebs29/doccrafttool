Purchase Orders

Purchase Order Number Guide

A purchase order number is the shared reference that connects approval, supply, delivery and payment records.

Use one unique reference

Every purchase order should have a unique number. Reusing numbers makes it difficult to know which order, delivery or invoice a record belongs to.

Choose a simple format

Sequential formats such as PO-0001 or 2026-PO-001 are easy to understand. Department codes can be useful, but avoid adding information that frequently changes.

Control who assigns numbers

Assign numbers through one register or system rather than allowing several people to invent them independently. This reduces duplicates and gaps.

Do not recycle cancelled numbers

Keep cancelled purchase orders in the register and mark their status. Reusing the number can create confusion if a supplier or invoice still references the cancelled order.

Share the number with the supplier

Ask the supplier to show the purchase-order number on order confirmations, delivery documents and invoices. This supports faster matching.

Maintain a register

Track the number, supplier, requester, approver, date, amount, status and related invoice. Even a small structured register can improve spending visibility.

Important: Document, accounting and tax requirements vary by location and circumstance. This guide provides general information and is not legal, tax or accounting advice.

Frequently asked questions

Can a PO number contain letters?

Yes. Prefixes such as PO or department codes are common.

Should PO numbers restart each year?

They can, especially with a year prefix, but the full identifier must remain unique.

Is the PO number created by the supplier?

No. The buyer normally assigns it.
